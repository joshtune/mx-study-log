Talk prep — Wunderli, 'Jesus Christ Is Not Our Burden; He Is Our Relief'

Step 1 of preparing Sunday's talk for Lehi 31st Ward — inventory the source talk before asking the Lord what to share.

talk-preplehi-31general-conferencechrist May 8, 2026 · Source: David J. Wunderli, April 2026 General Conference

Context

I’m speaking in Lehi 31st Ward sacrament meeting on Sunday, 2026-05-10. The assigned source is Elder David J. Wunderli’s April 2026 talk, Jesus Christ Is Not Our Burden; He Is Our Relief.

The job isn’t to retell the talk. The job is to read it carefully, then quietly ask the Lord what He wants the ward to hear from this body of teaching — and only deliver that. So this entry is step 1: a clean inventory of what’s actually in the talk, written down so I can pray over something concrete instead of vaguely.

My process

  1. Inventory (this entry) — doctrines, every scripture with context, stories, invitations, quotable lines.
  2. Sit with it — re-read the talk slowly with the inventory in front of me. Mark what tugs.
  3. Ask the Lord — fast/pray over which 1–2 threads He wants for this ward, this Sunday. Resist the urge to cover everything.
  4. Outline → write → memorize the structure, not the words.

A talk is a vehicle for the Spirit. The inventory is just so I’m not guessing what’s in the suitcase.

Doctrines taught (the talk’s spine)

  • Christ is the relief, not the weight. Removing Him from your life to feel lighter is the lie. The opposite is true.
  • Abiding with Christ. Daily discipleship — prayer, scripture, faith, repentance, covenant renewal — keeps Him present.
  • The Atonement as redemption and sustenance. He has overcome the world, suffered our afflictions, offers continual cleansing.
  • Worship as intention, not accident. Christ doesn’t drift into a life; He’s invited daily.
  • Satan’s deception of “ease.” The temptation to abandon Christ arrives dressed as relief. It is the lie.
  • Becoming like Him through walking with Him. Character, not just comfort, is the fruit.

Stories and analogies

  • The backpack on the hike. Wunderli’s six-year-old son packs only a picture of Jesus, then collects rocks along the trail. When his dad suggests removing the picture to lighten the load, the boy refuses. The picture isn’t the weight; the rocks are. The picture is what makes the weight bearable.
  • Joshua in West Africa. A newly-baptized 16-year-old, raised by a single mother in humble circumstances, summarizes what the gospel has done for his family in one word: relief.

Invitations Wunderli leaves

  • Keep Christ daily, on purpose — prayer, scripture (especially the Book of Mormon), faith, repentance, covenant renewal.
  • Don’t try to lighten your life by removing Him.
  • Walk with Him especially in the moments you feel least worthy or capable.
  • Let Him cleanse you as often as you need it. The well doesn’t run dry.

Lines worth carrying verbatim

  • “Jesus Christ is not the weight; He is the relief.”
  • “The enemy of happiness wants to separate you from Jesus Christ.”
  • “Satan is a liar. Jesus Christ is not the weight; He is the relief.”
  • “May we together continue to walk with Jesus Christ, even in our most difficult moments.”
  • “It is that simple.”

What’s tugging on me right now (pre-prayer)

Three threads I notice myself drawn to, just from reading. I’m flagging them so I can take them honestly to the Lord rather than confusing my preferences for the assignment:

  1. The deception that Christ is the weight. Specifically how this shows up for active members — not “I’m leaving the Church,” but the slow trade of daily discipleship for “I’m just busy.”
  2. “As often as my people repent.” The unlimited supply of second chances. This may be what someone in the congregation needs to hear more than the prevention message.
  3. The picture and the rocks. A clean, kid-told image that does theological work without sounding theological.

Next

Pray. Specifically: ask whether the ward needs the prevention message (“don’t put Him down”) or the return message (“come back, as often as needed”) — those land differently and I shouldn’t pick by taste.
